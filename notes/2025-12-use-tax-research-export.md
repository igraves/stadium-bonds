## Key Findings — Updated

### Primary Funding Engine (Unchanged Conclusion, Now Larger)
The financing is still overwhelmingly driven by **state sales tax growth captured via a massive STAR district**, but now we include compensating **use tax** which significantly boosts the base.

Previously (sales only, grocery adjusted):
- Sales tax state base ≈ **$495M/year**
- 30-year increment:
  - ≈ **$5.6B @ 2%**
  - ≈ **$9.4B @ 3%**

## New Analysis — Compensating Use Tax

### Use Tax Is Eligible
Under STAR bond statute, **state sales AND use tax** increment can be captured above a base year level.

### Kansas Rate Reality
- State use tax rate = state sales tax rate (6.5%)
- Local use tax rate = same as local sales tax rate

### KDOR Data Used
From CY 2025 KDOR Local Use Tax Distributions:
- Combined STAR-area local sales (cities + counties) ≈ **$380.4M**
- Combined STAR-area local use tax ≈ **$132.9M**

Therefore:
\""Use / Sales Ratio\" ≈ **34.9%**

---

## Boosted State Base

We previously established a **conservative**, grocery-adjusted **state sales base** of:
- **$495M/year**

Assuming use tax is largely non-grocery (reasonable and conservative), applying the 34.9% ratio gives:

- **State use tax base:** ≈ **$173M/year**

So combined state base is now:
> **$668M/year (sales + use)**

## Core Reality — Now Even Clearer

Alcohol and wagering streams are **minor coverage support tools**.

The **true funding engine** of this proposal is now clearly:

> A **$7.6B–$12.7B+ diversion of state sales + use tax growth** over 30 years, created by:
> - drawing an unprecedentedly large STAR district
> - capturing normal CPI-driven growth
> - layering capitalized interest and coverage ratio pledges
> - stretching repayment horizons

Even after:
- removing groceries from sales tax
- treating use as non-grocery (conservative)
- stacking only increment above base

The scale remains enormous, and even larger than originally discussed.

---

## Policy Framing

“It’s funded with gambling and booze” remains mathematically false.

Even accounting for LET, drink tax, and APSK:
- Together they maybe contribute **6–10%** of total STAR financing capacity.
- The overwhelming bulk of repayment capacity comes from **state revenue growth** that otherwise would have supported normal statewide government services.
